# The three zero-loop groups, their work and daily story

Status: **proposed character presentation/choreography**. Reuse the [shared anatomy stages](../ANATOMY-AND-ORGANIC-MOVEMENT.md); no characters, roles or face art are implemented here. This specification interprets the owner's requested black stick people and overlapping zero symbols without changing the existing cast's default faces in other themes.

## Body and identity layers

Use a centrally defined **stick-human silhouette style**: dark stick torso/neck, distinct upper/lower limbs, circular or oval head, readable palms/thumbs and heel/toe feet. Anatomy remains humanlike and contact-driven even when the artwork is thin. Do not bend a straight stick limb as rubber or use wrist dots in place of the new palm frame. Body shape, identity, age, appearance, face mark, group and job are separate fields, reusable by any theme.

Start with existing adult identities in engineering attire rather than duplicating them inside the theme: proposed Ridge at intake, Moss observing regulation, Sage at the calendar/display. These are initial casting choices, not fixed abilities or new identity definitions. Aster and the learner can visit the protected observation/teaching area; Pebble remains swaddled and supported if a family episode includes them. Learners do not operate power/pressure machinery or enter exposed work areas. Add shared engineering role kits before casting, instead of dressing every worker in a climbing harness.

Three groups distinguish community membership and visual mark; they are not ranks, inherent abilities or permanent jobs. Individuals may exchange tasks, help another group or work alone. Group labels and tool/position cues remain available when a tiny mark cannot be distinguished.

## Face-mark geometry

Working names: **One / Gatherers**, **Two / Keepers**, **Three / Makers**. Preserve the user's angular distinction:

```text
groupDelta = 1°, 2°, or 3°
full-detail loop count N = 12 (finite artistic sample of infinite zeroes)
loop k angle = baseAngle + (k - (N-1)/2) * groupDelta
all loops share a center, with an elliptical zero outline
```

The total spans are 11°, 22° and 33°, centered on the face. A circular head is fine, but circular loops rotated around their center would be indistinguishable; the symbol uses ellipses. It is an overlapping-zero motif, not automatically a mathematical infinity sign or the clock digit 0. The empty centers and negative space must remain readable.

Proposed head-relative geometry: ellipse width about 0.32 head heights, height about 0.58 head heights, centered slightly above head center, with a fine ivory/warm light stroke over the dark head. These are workshop starting proportions, not measured final art. Clip the motif inside the head with breathing room. Avoid so many opaque strokes that the center fills or the mark becomes a flickering blob.

Use elapsed time for an optional slow group rotation (initial study: one turn in 300 seconds), never “1° per rendered frame.” Incremental 1/2/3° is the **difference between loops**, not frame rate or physical clock accuracy. Head orientation follows the body; the symbol's local frame follows the head. Quiet mode uses a fixed mark. At smaller projected heads, retain a representative subset of loops and the same outer angular span; below reliable legibility, show a clean zero and a nearby group cue. Review the threshold visually rather than promising that 1° is distinguishable on a 7-inch display. Do not add production-only diagnostic labels to the everyday clock.

## Work roles and contacts

| Role/action | Human mechanics and real interface | Machine result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Crank operator | Two reachable rotating handles or one handle plus brace, open→grasp→push/pull→release, grounded stance and load-aware shoulder/pelvis shift | Work enters PW01/PW05; one-way clutch permits a supported rest without handle back-drive. |
| Treadle operator | Sole on AC05, hinged plate trajectory, knee/ankle reach and a separate planted support; slow stepping rather than floating feet | PW02 feeds the collector through its linkage and clutch. |
| Reserve keeper | Observe position gauge from walkway; inspect an isolated winding station, not a dangling load | Chooses when to replenish; reserve allows other people to rest. |
| Regulator observer | Stable lean, held timing/inspection tool, hands outside pendulum and escapement envelopes | Checks phase/rate in a study; does not force the pendulum faster to meet a deadline. |
| Calendar maker | Guides a supported sample module only when isolated; checks detent/setting marks | Demonstrates a month-length/carry lesson without manually changing the current date. |
| Hydraulic/air demonstrator | Uses reachable isolated controls, observes charge/flow, stays outside carriage/load gap | Shows energy conversion and loss, not an infinite self-power loop. |
| Repairer | Stop/isolate/support → inspect → replace/wipe → check → clear → re-engage | A service vignette; no fingers in live gears or hidden rescue of a falling mass. |
| Teacher/learner | Shared bench with disconnected gear pair, distinct grips and reciprocal pointing/listening | Learner predicts a ratio or notices a costly extra conversion. |
| Rest/help/meal | Hands release the machine before sitting, carrying food or helping another worker | Reserves keep the timing core running for their finite duration; people are valued beyond output. |

Names such as PW01/AC05 refer to the [object catalog](OBJECT-CATALOG.md). Tools include a reachable crank handle, inspection light, caliper/gauge, detached teaching gears, setting key, supported replacement cartridge and cleaning cloth. Their rendered grips, moving ends and inventories need shared definitions. No role carries every tool simultaneously.

## Busy scene with purposeful pauses

The default foreground budget is up to three active people, spaced on distinct platforms; further workers can belong to later chapters rather than being rendered as an overlapping crowd. One operator can work while another checks and a third rests. Machine motion comes from pendulum swings, correctly phased gears and bounded demonstrations, not random motion on every object. Continuous active-mode movement does not require a spinning month/year ring.

Eight hourly camera/story chapters reuse the same connected machine: intake platform, power reserve, regulator gallery, transmission hall, calendar bridge, hydraulic/air demonstration, service bench and shared overlook. Camera emphasis changes; shafts do not detach or rewire each hour. Keep the reading panel stable and current while the background viewpoint drifts gently. Still-camera mode suppresses that drift without changing the time model.

Minute vignettes can use prepare → approach → grasp → work → release → recover → help/teach, fitted to available time without delaying digits. Second beats are the escapement's job; people do not sprint to flip each second. At minute/hour/midnight carries, the release/indexer is visibly connected and the register shutter handles finite motion. Actors respond to the event without claiming their last-second gesture sets the device clock.

## Daily philosophy and episode variants

| Daily strand | Episode | Realization |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Alone | A maker adds another conversion and discovers extra losses. They remove unnecessary work and watch the pendulum. | More motion is not always more useful work. |
| Family | A caregiver and learner visit the safe gallery while the shift takes a meal. | The reserve serves life outside the machine. |
| Helping | One group shares a tool and takes a short input shift so another can rest. | Continuity belongs to cooperation, not a single exhausted worker. |
| Refusal and repair | Someone initially keeps a spare to themselves; a later delay leads to lending it and rebuilding trust. | Efficiency without care can create more work. |
| Teaching | An apprentice questions whether the air demonstration consumes more energy than it saves. The mentor measures and listens. | Understanding includes permission to revise a beautiful idea. |
| Long view | The crew marks a renewed cord/filter cartridge and passes a service drawing to a younger observer. | A long-lived machine is supported by knowledge and care, not a promise that nothing ever wears. |

A quiet daily realization has people set down tools and look outward while stored energy supports the clock. These are authored reconstructable episodes, not simulated births, aging, deaths or hereditary groups. The face loops evoke continuity and shared time without making characters interchangeable.

## Visual composition and readability

Full-screen background: warm brass/steel wheels, muted blue hydraulic paths, pale air paths, dark frame and occasional clear inspection windows. Put black figures against lighter working platforms/local backplates so their hands and feet remain legible. Do not rely on color alone: line style, port symbols, labels in study views and geometry distinguish energy/control paths.

At 854×480, the main HH:MM occupies the protected central upper reading area; seconds sit adjacent at a smaller, steady scale and year/month/day form a separate line. Gear trains surround or sit behind a low-detail glass-reading region. Input and rest platforms occupy lower/side areas without crossing current numerals. Desktop can reveal more cutaway detail; portrait stacks display and machine modules while preserving the same connections. Size/contrast and the minimum readable face motif need actual native-size review in E4/E5. Do not solve crowding by hiding required time fields or shrinking people into illegible dots.

Reduced motion shows a complete settled mechanism with supported figures and fixed face marks. Hidden/Settings/screen-rest behavior follows the current app. Companions off hides people and their tools while leaving an honest authored machine snapshot; do not imply new human work is entering the reserve while no people are present. Accessible time is text, not an announcement for every gear or 1 Hz live-region update.
